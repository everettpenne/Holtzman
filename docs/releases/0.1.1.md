# Holtzman 0.1.1 (pre-release)

A camera for the Glass suite, built for the Google Pixel 10a. Installs over 0.1.0 (same signing key) and beside the older fork build (`com.everettpenne.holtzman.dev`).

**What changed since 0.1.0**
- **The burst is the normal shot.** Pressing the shutter merges six RAW frames on the GPU and saves one JPEG, about 1.5 to 1.9 seconds. The BURST chip turns it off for a quick single frame; the RAW chip adds the merged DNG. A toggle in the probe sheet also saves the unmerged frame, for comparison.
- **A better picture.** A directional demosaic (about 2 dB better than before on a test with known answers), a spatial denoise of the merged RAW guided by the camera's measured noise, a noise-aware finish (denoise, detail, local contrast), local tone mapping, light film grain, and a tone curve chosen per picture. Tuned against the Pixel camera app's pictures of two scenes: on the same scene the brightness distribution and local contrast come out close to its. **Ultra HDR** (on by default; the HDR chip) is built from the merged frame.
- **Faces and focus.** Focus and metering go to the largest detected face, otherwise the middle of the frame (tap to focus still wins); a face is kept out of the shadows and highlights in the tone curve without evening out skin tones.
- **Shake-aware exposure.** In dim light with an unsteady hand, exposure is cut and ISO raised to keep each frame sharp; the merge absorbs the noise.
- **Dataset mode** (the DATA chip): saves RAW frames and metadata (handheld burst, 40-frame clean reference, ISO ladder) to `Documents/Holtzman/Dataset` for tuning.
- **Fixes:** burst photos no longer show rotated a quarter turn in the gallery (the rotation tag is now written before the file is published); two bursts in a row no longer run the app out of memory; bursts use the preview's focus distance instead of an undefined one.

**Read before installing**
- Tested on one phone: a Pixel 10a on GrapheneOS (Android 17). Nothing else has been tried.
- **Not yet tried:** the face path with a real face in view, and the shake-aware exposure in a shaky hand in the dark. Both are in because their parts test correctly, not because they have been seen working in use.
- Tuned on two scenes (a dim room and a bright indoor desk). Bright outdoor scenes, backlit scenes and people are unverified.
- Everything runs on the CPU and GPU: the Tensor NPU is not reachable for apps like this one (see docs/ix-pipeline.md).
- If a GPU stage fails to build on a device the app falls back to simpler processing instead of crashing, but the picture will be plainer.
